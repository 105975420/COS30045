# TV Energy Insights

## Data Story

### Overview

TV Energy Insights explores how television screen size and screen technology relate to annual energy consumption. The website presents data visualisations to help Australian consumers understand these patterns and make more informed TV purchasing decisions.

### Target Audience

The target audience is Australian consumers who are planning to purchase a television. They may have different levels of technical knowledge but are interested in comparing televisions and understanding their energy consumption.

### Audience Interests

The website focuses on three key questions:

- How does screen size relate to annual energy consumption?
- How does average energy consumption differ across screen technologies?
- What should consumers consider when comparing televisions?

### Key Findings

The scatter plot shows a general trend that larger TVs tend to consume more energy annually, although consumption varies between TVs of similar sizes.

The bar chart shows the average annual energy consumption for three screen technologies in the analysed dataset:

- LCD: 332.30 kWh/year
- LCD (LED): 460.20 kWh/year
- OLED: 488.70 kWh/year

These results describe the analysed dataset and should not be treated as universal values for every television model.

## About the Data

### Data Source

The analysis uses a television dataset containing information about screen size, screen technology and labelled annual energy consumption.

### Data Processing

The dataset was processed in KNIME before creating the visualisations. The analysis used 4,508 rows and selected five columns for the relevant data processing and visualisation tasks. Screen size was converted to inches for the scatter plot.

Two visualisations were created:

1. A scatter plot to explore the relationship between screen size and annual energy consumption.
2. A bar chart to compare average annual energy consumption across screen technologies.

### Accuracy and Limitations

The visualisations represent patterns in the available dataset. The results may not represent all televisions currently available in Australia.

The observed differences may be influenced by other television characteristics, including screen size differences between technology groups. The analysis identifies relationships and differences but does not establish causation.

### Privacy and Ethics

The analysis focuses on television product characteristics and energy consumption rather than personal information. The visualisations are intended to communicate the data clearly and avoid misleading conclusions. Findings are presented with appropriate limitations to help consumers interpret the results responsibly.
